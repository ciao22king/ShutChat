ShutChat is a messenger with no server and no account. Two devices find each other, agree on a key, and talk directly. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to hand over: no phone number, no email, no profile.

HOW IT WORKS

You create a chat and get a short code: 24 characters in Crockford Base32, generated from a 15-byte invite. You give that code to the other person however you like, in person, in a message, or as a QR code. When they enter it, the two devices connect and start talking.

Messages and files are encrypted end to end. The key never leaves the two devices.

THREE WAYS TO REACH SOMEONE

- Local network: the devices find each other on the same Wi-Fi, with UDP discovery and TCP for the data.
- Nearby: Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct when there is no network at all.
- Relay: a public MQTT broker carries encrypted frames when the two devices are on different networks, so distance stops mattering. The broker sees only ciphertext and cannot read a message, a name or a call.

WHAT YOU CAN SEND

- Text messages, with replies, edits and delivery state.
- Photos and files of any size, sent in chunks with a progress bar.
- Voice notes.
- Audio calls, with mute, hang up and a call timer.

PRIVACY

- No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising ID.
- No account, no contact upload, no address book access.
- Chats live in the app's own storage. You can archive them, set them to expire, and delete them completely.
- The code is open source under Apache-2.0, and you can build it yourself.

REQUIREMENTS

Android 8.0 or newer. The nearby and relay transports use optional Google components; the local network transport needs none of them. There is also a Linux desktop client that talks to the same chats, so a phone and a computer can message each other.

ShutChat is for people who want a conversation that belongs to them and nobody else. It makes no promises about magic: it is honest about what a relay sees, what a LAN can guess, and what happens when a device is offline.
